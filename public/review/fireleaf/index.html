What Google sees

Six things holding your search traffic back

  • High

    Products sit inside Dutchie's frame

    The shop pages on fireleaf.com carry a heading and little else. The products load from Dutchie's address, so that is where Google files them.

  • High

    Categories have no content of their own

    Every category shows the same heading and no products in the page itself, so it is unlikely to rank for searches like "edibles Brooklyn".

  • High

    Your old site is still live

    fireleafny.com is still indexed, with shop categories, brand pages and blog posts. It still says "Delivery coming soon", and it competes with fireleaf.com for your own name. Redirecting it to fireleaf.com and moving the blog posts keeps its search value and sends visitors to the current site.

  • Medium

    Brand links send value to dutchie.com

    Your featured brands (Cheech & Chong, Hudson Cannabis, High Peaks and others) link to Dutchie's embedded pages instead of brand pages on your site.

  • Medium

    No location in the page basics

    The homepage has no meta description. The title is "FireLeaf dispensary" and the main heading is "Fast, Discreet Delivery Available", with no mention of Brooklyn or your neighborhood.

  • Medium

    A name you share with another chain

    Searches for "Fire Leaf dispensary" also surface a dispensary chain in Oklahoma. Pages that tie your brand to Brooklyn and your address help Google tell the two apart.
