The four Dutchie menu setups — and which one actually ranks

Embedded menu (default iFrame): only partially indexed by Google. It can drive some traffic, but product URLs and schema don’t live on your domain — a ceiling you’ll hit fast.

Dutchie Pro + Proxy / Dutchie Pro: serves your storefront as part of your root domain, fully crawlable with real product URLs and schema. This is the configuration that ranks — and what we recommend for most dispensaries serious about organic.

Subdomain menu: fully indexed, but Google treats a subdomain as a separate site — so authority, local relevance, and internal links don’t fully flow to your main domain. We generally avoid it. Dutchie+: a fully custom, indexable build for larger MSOs. We help you pick and configure the right one.

    Dutchie menu SEO FAQ

    Can Google actually index my Dutchie menu?

    It depends on your configuration. The default embedded iFrame is only partially indexed, while Dutchie Pro (root-domain) and Dutchie+ serve fully crawlable product pages. We audit what's indexed today and fix how your menu is served so pages can be crawled.

    Why aren't my Dutchie products showing up in search?

    Usually because the menu is embedded in a cross-origin iFrame Google doesn't crawl as part of your page, there's no Product schema on your domain, and product pages are thin or duplicated. Those are exactly the issues a menu SEO engagement fixes.

    What's the best Dutchie setup for SEO?

    For most dispensaries, Dutchie Pro (or Pro + Proxy) is best because your menu is served on your root domain with real product URLs and schema. We generally avoid a subdomain-only menu, since Google treats subdomains as separate sites.

    Do I need Dutchie Pro to do menu SEO?

    You can win some ground on an embedded menu, but the ceiling is low. Real product-level ranking needs indexable pages on your domain — which is why we often pair menu SEO with a move to Dutchie Pro.

    Will a subdomain menu hurt my SEO?

    It's better than a fully hidden iFrame, but Google treats a subdomain as a separate site, so authority, local relevance, and internal links don't fully flow to your main domain. Root-domain configurations consolidate that value instead.

    Can you optimize product pages for specific strains and products?

    Yes. We give categories and high-value products unique, compliant descriptions, add product schema, and build category hubs — while fixing duplication so similar products don't compete with each other.

    How does menu SEO help with AI search?

    AI assistants recommend and cite from clear, crawlable, structured pages. Indexable product pages with schema are what make your inventory eligible to be the answer when someone asks an assistant for a product near them.
