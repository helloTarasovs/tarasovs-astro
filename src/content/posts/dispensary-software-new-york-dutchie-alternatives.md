<p><strong>Dutchie is the default for many New York stores</strong>, partly because the state chose it as the main POS and e-commerce provider for the first CAURD retailers in 2022. Treez, Flowhub, Cova, BLAZE, Meadow and Jane are the alternatives most often compared with it. Before you switch, check live Metrc integration in New York and where your menu will live, because that decides whether Google can index your products.</p>

<h2 id="ta-ny-context">What Is Specific to New York</h2>
<p>Two facts shape software choices in New York more than any feature list.</p>
<h3>Metrc is the state tracking system</h3>
<p>New York moved its seed-to-sale tracking from BioTrack to Metrc. According to the Office of Cannabis Management's seed-to-sale bulletins, retail licensees had to be credentialed in Metrc by December 17, 2025, and the deadline for entering existing retail inventory was extended to January 12, 2026. Every sale now has to reach Metrc, either through Metrc directly or through a POS that integrates with its API.</p>
<p>In practice, that makes Metrc integration the first filter. A POS that works well in another state is not automatically ready for New York's rules, Retail Item ID labels and reporting.</p>
<h3>Many early stores started on Dutchie</h3>
<p>In December 2022, Dutchie announced that New York State had selected it as the main point-of-sale and e-commerce provider for Conditional Adult-Use Retail Dispensary (CAURD) licensees. Trade press reported that the service was offered to those licensees at no cost. That is why so many of the first New York dispensaries run on Dutchie today, and why "Dutchie alternatives" is a common search among NY operators who are now reviewing their stack.</p>
<div class="ta-note">Terms, integrations and deadlines change. Confirm current Metrc requirements with OCM and current contract terms with each vendor before you decide.</div>

<h2 id="ta-demo">Questions to Ask in Every Demo</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Is your Metrc integration live for New York retailers today?</strong> Ask how many New York stores use it.</li>
<li><strong>How are sales reported to Metrc?</strong> Real time or in batches, what happens when a call fails, and how the system retries and reconciles counts.</li>
<li><strong>Do you handle Retail Item ID QR codes</strong> at receiving and at checkout?</li>
<li><strong>Where does the online menu live?</strong> On your domain, a subdomain, or inside an embed from the vendor's site?</li>
<li><strong>Does each product get its own indexable URL</strong> with product schema on a domain you control?</li>
<li><strong>What happens to existing menu URLs if you migrate?</strong> Can you set 301 redirects?</li>
<li><strong>Can you export your customer, sales and product data</strong> if you leave later?</li>
<li><strong>What is the outage history on peak days</strong> such as 4/20, and what is the support response time?</li>
</ol>
<p>Questions 4 to 6 are the ones most buyers skip, and they are the ones that decide organic traffic for the next several years.</p>
<h2 id="ta-seo">The Menu Question That Decides Your SEO</h2>
<p>Your menu holds every product a customer could search for by name. If that menu is embedded from another domain, search engines and AI assistants do not treat those products as part of your website. You end up with a store that has hundreds of products and almost none of them in Google.</p>
<p>The fix is not always a new POS. On Dutchie, for example, moving from an embedded menu to a storefront on your own domain changes how the products are indexed. When we moved <a href="/verdi-cannabis-dutchie-pro-case-study/">Verdi Cannabis in New York to Dutchie E-Commerce Pro</a>, organic clicks from Google grew by 64% and impressions by four times, with around 900 product pages indexed (Search Console, third month after the February 2026 migration compared with the 90 days before).</p>
<p>Whatever platform you choose, the same rules apply:</p>
<ul>
<li>Keep the menu on your domain or a path under it.</li>
<li>Give categories and products clean, crawlable URLs.</li>
<li>Add product schema from live inventory.</li>
<li>Link the menu from your local and blog pages.</li>
<li>Plan redirects before any migration, not after.</li>
</ul>
