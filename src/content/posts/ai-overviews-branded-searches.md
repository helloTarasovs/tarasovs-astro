<div class="ta-qa-label">Quick Answer</div>
<p><strong>When a buyer searches for your company on Google, the first thing they see may no longer be your website. More and more often, it is an AI Overview: a short summary Google writes about your brand, built from pages Google chose.</strong></p>
<p>In Ahrefs' data, AI Overviews appeared on 82.91% of US desktop results for branded keywords on September 29, 2026. Ahrefs' November 2025 study, which used a different sample, put the figure at 13.1%. The two numbers are not a perfect like-for-like comparison, but the direction is clear.</p>

<div class="ta-body">
<p>For years, the brand search was the one result nobody worried about. Someone typed your name and found your site. That is no longer a safe assumption. Below is what the data shows, why it matters for your business, and how to check what Google's AI says about your company.</p>

<h2 id="ta-data">What the data shows</h2>
<p>AI Overviews now appear on branded searches almost as often as on generic ones. Ahrefs analyzed 232.8 million US desktop search results from July to September 2026, including 92.5 million branded-keyword results. "Branded keywords" here means any search that contains a brand name, such as <code>[brand] login</code> or <code>[brand] pricing</code>, not only the bare company name.</p>

<p>The gap between branded and non-branded searches shrank from 12.2 points in July to 5 points in September.</p>
<p>Ahrefs also tracked exact-name searches for a separate panel of 100 large global brands from the Interbrand list. On July 1, only 5 of 98 usable results had an AI Overview. By September 30, 63 of 100 did. In 48 of those 63 cases (76.2%), the AI Overview sat in position one, above the brand's own website. Keep in mind that this panel covers some of the best-known brands in the world. Small and local businesses may see different patterns.</p>

<div class="ta-note">Two limits are worth keeping in mind. The data is US desktop only. And Google changes when and where AI Overviews appear all the time, so treat these numbers as a snapshot, not a constant.</div>

<h2 id="ta-why">Why this matters for your business</h2>
<p>The bigger risk is not lost clicks. It is losing control of the first thing a buyer reads about you.</p>
<p><strong>Someone else writes your first impression.</strong> A prospect doing due diligence searches your name before a call. If an AI Overview sits at the top of the page, that summary is their first impression, and Google assembled it from whatever sources it judged relevant for that search.</p>
<p><strong>Those sources can be out of date.</strong> Wikipedia, YouTube and LinkedIn are hard for a company to control. SEO consultant Ann Smarty pointed out that videos cited in brand AI Overviews often come from accounts the brand does not run and may contain outdated product information. For a B2B company, that can mean old pricing, a retired product, a former executive, or a compliance claim that is no longer accurate. In security and enterprise sales, a wrong certification in the summary can raise doubts before anyone visits your site.</p>
<p><strong>Clicks may change.</strong> In a separate study, Ahrefs found a 58% lower click-through rate for the top-ranking page when an AI Overview was present. That figure comes from a broad dataset and is not a forecast for any one company's brand traffic. For brand searches, accuracy usually matters more than traffic. A buyer who reads a correct summary and still books a demo is fine. A buyer who reads a wrong one may not call at all.</p>

<h2 id="ta-first-check">Start with a 15-minute first check</h2>
<p>You do not need a tool to start. Pick three searches that matter most to buyers: your bare company name, <code>[brand] pricing</code> and <code>[brand] reviews</code>.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Search like a buyer would.</strong> Use a private window, logged out. This reduces personalization from your account, but it does not change your physical location. If your buyers are in another country, keep in mind that they may see different results.</li>
<li><strong>Note what appears.</strong> Is there an AI Overview? Where does it sit on the page? Which sources does it cite?</li>
<li><strong>Read every claim.</strong> Does it get your pricing, products and key facts right? Mark anything wrong, missing or out of date.</li>
<li><strong>Take a dated screenshot.</strong> You will want it later to see whether anything changed.</li>
</ol>
<p>If all three answers are accurate, you can stop here and repeat the check next month. If you found problems, move on to the full audit.</p>

<h2 id="ta-audit">The full audit</h2>
<p>The full audit covers more queries and takes longer, usually an hour or two depending on how many problems you find.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Expand to about 10 queries.</strong> Add the questions buyers actually ask: <code>[brand] vs [competitor]</code>, <code>[brand] alternatives</code>, <code>is [brand] legit</code>, your main product names, and, for B2B, <code>[brand] security</code> or <code>[brand] SOC 2</code>.</li>
<li><strong>Record your setup.</strong> Write down the date, country or location, language and device for each session. Results vary across all four, so this makes later checks comparable.</li>
<li><strong>Check every claim.</strong> Compare each statement against reality: pricing, products, founding year, leadership, locations, integrations, certifications.</li>
<li><strong>Open the cited pages.</strong> Check whether they actually support the disputed claim. A citation may not reveal the exact origin of an error. If no cited page supports it, record that and submit feedback.</li>
<li><strong>Send feedback on clear errors.</strong> Use the feedback option on the AI Overview. It does not guarantee a change, but it puts the error on record.</li>
<li><strong>Repeat monthly.</strong> Run the audit again after any pricing change, rebrand, product launch or leadership change.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="ta-change">What you can actually change</h2>
<p>You cannot edit an AI Overview. You can improve the information it draws from, though fixing a source does not guarantee the summary will change.</p>
<p>Google's own guide explains the mechanics. Its AI features retrieve pages from the regular Search index and run extra related searches in the background, a process Google calls query fan-out. So a search for your brand name may also pull results for your pricing, reviews and alternatives. If you have no page that answers one of those questions, Google has to rely on someone else's.</p>
<p>What that means in practice:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Answer brand questions on your own site.</strong> Keep a clear About page with plain facts: what you do, who it is for, when you started, where you are based. Publish pricing if you can. Keep product, integration and security or compliance pages current.</li>
<li><strong>Stay eligible.</strong> Google says a page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet to appear in its AI features. Your site must also be included under Settings &gt; Search generative AI in Search Console, which is the default. A <code>nosnippet</code> or <code>max-snippet:0</code> rule on a page stops Google from using that page's content as a direct input for AI Overviews and AI Mode, and it also removes the regular snippet.</li>
<li><strong>Keep your Google Business Profile accurate.</strong> Google names it as a source for local business details in AI responses.</li>
<li><strong>Fix the third-party pages you can.</strong> Update your LinkedIn company page and directory listings. On Wikipedia, suggest corrections on the article's talk page rather than editing it yourself, since Wikipedia's conflict-of-interest rules apply.</li>
<li><strong>Publish your own current video.</strong> If an old third-party video is being cited, a recent product overview on your own YouTube channel gives Google a better option. You can also ask the creator of an outdated video to add a correction.</li>
</ul>
<p>And what Google says you can skip for its search features: llms.txt files, special AI markup, chopping content into small chunks, rewriting pages "for AI", and chasing fake mentions. Structured data is still worth having for rich results, but Google says no special schema is needed for its AI features. For the full evidence on the first of those, see our <a href="/llms-txt-guide/">llms.txt implementation guide</a>.</p>
<div class="ta-note">One caveat. All of this applies to Google. ChatGPT, Perplexity and other assistants pick sources differently, so a brand that looks fine in Google can still be described poorly elsewhere. See <a href="/how-to-rank-in-chatgpt-search/">how to rank in ChatGPT Search</a> for that side.</div>

<h2 id="ta-track">How to track it over time</h2>
<p>The manual audit tells you what the summary says. Search Console tells you how often your site's links appear.</p>
<p>Search Console's Generative AI performance report for Search tracks impressions of links to your site in AI Overviews and AI Mode. Discover has a separate generative AI report. These reports measure your site's visibility, not every AI answer that mentions your brand. If you do not see the report at all, your site may not have enough impressions in these features yet.</p>
<p>Check the report once a month and record the total, your top pages and the country split. Then look at branded queries in the main Performance report. If branded impressions stay stable while clicks fall, investigate changes in rankings, query mix, devices and other search features alongside AI Overview visibility. The trend alone does not prove that AI Overviews caused the decline.</p>
<p>Search Console will not show you what the summary says. That is why the manual check still matters. Third-party tools can add scale, but Google warns that no outside tool has access to its internal ranking or AI systems, so judge their claims carefully.</p>

<h2>FAQ</h2>
<div class="ta-faq-list">
<div class="ta-faq-item"><p class="ta-faq-question">Can I turn off AI Overviews for searches of my brand?</p><p class="ta-faq-answer">You cannot prevent Google from generating an AI Overview about your brand using other sources. You can exclude your own site's links and content through Settings &gt; Search generative AI in Search Console. Google says this control does not affect ranking or inclusion elsewhere in Search. Content from other sites will still be used, so excluding your site usually leaves the summary to third parties.</p></div>
<div class="ta-faq-item"><p class="ta-faq-question">What do nosnippet and max-snippet do?</p><p class="ta-faq-answer"><code>nosnippet</code> and <code>max-snippet:0</code> stop Google from using a page's content as a direct input for AI Overviews and AI Mode. A positive <code>max-snippet</code> value only limits how much text can be used. Both rules also affect your regular search snippets, and neither stops Google from mentioning your brand based on other sources.</p></div>
<div class="ta-faq-item"><p class="ta-faq-question">Can I correct a wrong AI Overview directly?</p><p class="ta-faq-answer">Not directly. Make sure your own site states the correct fact clearly, update the third-party pages you can, and use the feedback option on the AI Overview.</p></div>
<div class="ta-faq-item"><p class="ta-faq-question">Does this apply outside the US?</p><p class="ta-faq-answer">The Ahrefs numbers are US desktop only. AI Overviews are live in many other countries, so run the check in each market where you sell.</p></div>
<div class="ta-faq-item"><p class="ta-faq-question">Is optimizing for AI Overviews different from SEO?</p><p class="ta-faq-answer">For Google, no. Google states that optimizing for its generative AI features is still SEO. Other AI assistants, such as ChatGPT and Perplexity, choose sources differently and need separate checks.</p></div>
<div class="ta-faq-item"><p class="ta-faq-question">How often should I check?</p><p class="ta-faq-answer">Run the first check monthly. Run the full audit after any change to pricing, products, leadership or branding, or when the first check turns up problems.</p></div>
