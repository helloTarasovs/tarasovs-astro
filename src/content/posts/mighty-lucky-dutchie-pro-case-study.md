<h2 id="ta-challenge">The Challenge</h2>
<p>Mighty Lucky's menu ran as an embedded Dutchie iframe on a single Shopify page. Customers could shop, but the products didn't have their own pages on the site. Google picked up only some of them, through parameter-based URLs on that one menu page, and did so inconsistently. Those URLs had no product titles, descriptions or structured data of their own, so the menu added little to the dispensary's search visibility.</p>
<h2 id="ta-work">What We Did</h2>
<h3>Moved the menu to E-Commerce Pro</h3>
<p>The full store now lives on the dispensary's own domain at mighty-lucky.com/stores/mighty-lucky, with a separate, indexable page for every product, category and brand.</p>
<h3>Built a custom storefront with the Dutchie SDK</h3>
<p>We developed a custom theme with a branded header, footer and colors, so the store looks and feels like the rest of the Mighty Lucky website rather than a third-party menu.</p>
<h3>Protected existing search traffic</h3>
<p>We redirected old menu and category URLs to their new equivalents and pointed the remaining old product URLs to the store's product catalog, so visitors and search engines didn't hit dead ends.</p>
<h3>Set up the proxy and the domain</h3>
<p>We configured the proxy and made the DNS changes needed to serve the store on the main domain, while Dutchie activated Pro on its side.</p>
<h3>Checked indexing after launch</h3>
<p>We monitored Google Search Console to confirm the new store pages were being crawled and showing in search.</p>
<h2 id="ta-results">The Results</h2>
<p>In the first four weeks after launch, Mighty Lucky's daily search clicks averaged 148, up from 92 in the pre-launch baseline, an increase of 61%.</p>
<p>More than 820 dedicated product pages now appear in Google search results. Before the migration, Google showed about 400 parameter-based menu URLs, picked up inconsistently. Clicks to product pages roughly doubled, from about 12 to 26 per day.</p>
<p>Store pages earned 55,700 impressions in their first five weeks, with a median position of 7.8. Many of these searches are for specific products and strains rather than the dispensary's name, which means new customers are finding the store through what it sells.</p>
<div class="ta-note"><strong>Methodology:</strong> data from Google Search Console. The baseline period is August 9 to September 10, 2026, which excludes a one-off seasonal traffic spike in September. Clicks from the Google Business Profile link, which now points to the store, are not counted as new product traffic. Part of the overall growth also reflects ongoing content work on the site.</div>
<h2 id="ta-takeaways">Key Takeaways</h2>
<p>An embedded menu lets customers buy, but it doesn't give products their own place in search. Moving to E-Commerce Pro turns the menu into hundreds of pages that can rank on the dispensary's own domain. With the Dutchie SDK, the store can also match the brand instead of looking like a third-party add-on.</p>
