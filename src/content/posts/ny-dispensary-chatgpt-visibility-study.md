<div class="ta-qa__label">Quick Answer</div>
<p><strong>We ran 1,000 recorded ChatGPT prompts across 100 licensed New York dispensaries. Fifty scored 70 or above. Thirty-one scored under 40. Nine were not named in a single one of their ten prompts.</strong></p>
<p>The scores do not cluster around the middle. There is a visible group and an invisible group, and only 19 of the 100 sit between them. That shape matters more than any individual score, because the observed distribution behaves more like a visibility threshold than a conventional ranking curve.</p>
<p>This post reports what the data shows. It deliberately does not claim to explain why any single dispensary scored what it did, and the last section is about the limits of that.</p>

<h2 id="what">What we measured</h2>
<p>Every dispensary in the <a href="/dispensary-visibility/">New York Dispensary Visibility Index</a> gets the same treatment: ten non-branded local discovery prompts, each entered in a separate new ChatGPT Temporary Chat with no prior conversation history, each response recorded. Six prompts cover the dispensary's own market, two the surrounding market, two the wider metro or region. The composite score weights those three levels 70, 20 and 10 percent.</p>
<p>The prompts are the kind a customer actually types before deciding where to go. "What are the best cannabis dispensaries in Kenmore, New York." "Where can I buy legal cannabis in Malone, New York." Nothing branded, nothing leading.</p>
<p>A score is published only when all ten prompts return a valid recorded result. Positions are read from the response, not inferred from Google, directories or our own judgment. The full method, including the points table, is documented on the <a href="/new-york-dispensary-ai-visibility-index/">Index methodology page</a>, and every profile publishes its ten prompts individually so any score can be checked rather than taken on trust.</p>
<p>These 100 are the first to complete the benchmark. New York had 705 licensed dispensaries open as of August 2026, so this is a sample, and the section on limits below is blunt about what kind of sample it is.</p>

<p>Fifty dispensaries at 70 or above. Thirty-one under 40. Nineteen in the entire middle range between them. The mean is 55.0 and the median is 69.5, and the gap between those two numbers is itself a signal: a long tail of very low scores is dragging the average well below the typical result.</p>
<p>A ranking spread across a hundred businesses would normally put most results near the middle with thinner tails at each end. These results do the opposite: the two ends are heavy and the middle is hollow. We have not run a formal test of normality, and with a non-random sample of 100 that would not settle much anyway. What can be said is descriptive and still useful: the scores do not cluster around the middle.</p>
<p>The practical reading is that appearing in an AI recommendation behaves more like a threshold than a ranking. That is a different problem from ranking tenth on Google instead of third. On Google, tenth still exists on the page. Below this line, the customer never learns the business exists.</p>

<h2 id="seventy">The trap at exactly 70</h2>
<p>Eleven dispensaries scored exactly 70. In a 0 to 100 range that is a suspicious cluster, so we checked the underlying records rather than assuming a rounding artifact.</p>
<p>It is not an artifact. It is a signature. All eleven share the same profile: first place in every one of the six prompts in their own market, and absent from all four prompts covering the surrounding and wider markets. That produces 100 for the primary level, zero for the other two, and a weighted composite of exactly 70.</p>

Density seems to concentrate visibility rather than distribute it.</p>
<p>We are describing this, not explaining it. A plausible reading is that in a crowded market the model has more candidates than it can name and falls back on the businesses with the strongest external footprint, but this dataset cannot confirm that, and the next section explains why.</p>
<h2 id="limits">What this does not tell you</h2>
<p>Four limits, stated plainly, because a study that only lists its findings is not much use.</p>
<h3>The sample is not random</h3>
<p>These are the first 100 dispensaries to complete the benchmark, not a random draw from the 705 licensed retailers open in New York. Selection followed the order in which we worked through the list. That is fine for describing the shape of results within this group, and not fine for asserting a precise statewide figure. When someone says "31 percent of New York dispensaries are invisible in ChatGPT," that is a claim this dataset does not support. What it supports is that 31 of the 100 measured so far scored under 40.</p>
<h3>Some cuts are too small to mean anything</h3>
<p>Manhattan at 18 profiles is usable. The Bronx at four and Staten Island at three are not. We have deliberately not published borough-by-borough medians for those, because with samples that size a single business moves the number several points. Comparisons here are limited to Manhattan against the rest of the sample, and New York City against the rest of the state.</p>
<h3>This is correlation-free reporting</h3>
<p>This post does not explain why any dispensary scored what it did. We recorded what ChatGPT returned. We did not, in this round, systematically collect the variables that would let anyone test a cause: Google Business Profile completeness, review volume and recency, third-party editorial coverage, menu indexability, name and address consistency across directories. Until those are gathered against the same 100 businesses, any explanation offered here would be a hypothesis wearing the costume of a finding. Collecting them is the next piece of work.</p>
<h3>Every score is a dated snapshot</h3>
<p>ChatGPT responses vary with the model, the date, the available sources and the exact wording. Each profile carries the date it was checked. A score is evidence of what a standardized prompt returned on that day, not a permanent ranking, and profiles are rechecked on a rolling basis. Some numbers in this post will already have moved by the time you read it, which is why the underlying profiles rather than this article are the source of record.</p>
<div class="ta-callout">
<strong>Why we are publishing scores of businesses we do not work with, including low ones.</strong> Tarasovs Digital Agency sells dispensary search services, so we have an obvious interest in a study that shows a visibility problem. That is worth stating rather than burying. Our answer is method rather than assurance: every business is measured with the same ten prompts and the same scoring, every prompt result is published so any score can be checked, and profiles go live whether the number is 99 or zero. Any owner who thinks their result is wrong can request a recheck and we will re-run the full prompt set.

<div class="ta-faq__item">
<h3 class="ta-faq__q">How many New York dispensaries were measured, and how?</h3>
<p class="ta-faq__a">One hundred licensed New York dispensaries, each tested with the same ten non-branded local discovery prompts, for 1,000 recorded prompts in total. Each prompt was entered in a separate new ChatGPT Temporary Chat with no prior conversation history and the response was recorded. Six prompts cover the dispensary's own market, two the surrounding market and two the wider region, weighted 70, 20 and 10 percent into a composite score from 0 to 100.</p>
</div>
<div class="ta-faq__item">
<h3 class="ta-faq__q">What does it mean that 31 dispensaries scored under 40?</h3>
<p class="ta-faq__a">It means that across ten standardized prompts, those businesses were rarely or never named in the recorded ChatGPT responses. It is a measure of recommendation visibility on the check date, not a judgment about product quality, service or the business itself. Thirty-one of the 100 measured so far fall in that band, which is a statement about this sample rather than about all 705 licensed dispensaries in New York.</p>
</div>
<div class="ta-faq__item">
<h3 class="ta-faq__q">Why did eleven dispensaries score exactly 70?</h3>
<p class="ta-faq__a">All eleven share the same pattern: first place in all six prompts covering their own market, and not named in any of the four prompts covering the surrounding and wider markets. That produces 100 for the primary market, zero for the other two levels, and a weighted composite of exactly 70. It is a real pattern rather than a scoring artifact, and it describes a business that is visible only to customers who already name its immediate neighbourhood.</p>
</div>
<div class="ta-faq__item">
<h3 class="ta-faq__q">Does a zero score mean the dispensary does not exist online?</h3>
<p class="ta-faq__a">No. It means that on the check date, across ten standardized prompts, the business was not named in any recorded response. A dispensary can have a working website, an active Google Business Profile and strong local search rankings and still score zero here, because AI recommendation and traditional local search run on different inputs. Owners can request a recheck of any profile at any time.</p>
</div>
<div class="ta-faq__item">
<h3 class="ta-faq__q">Why does Manhattan perform worse than upstate markets?</h3>
<p class="ta-faq__a">Across the 18 Manhattan dispensaries measured the median composite is 36, against 70 across the other 82 in the sample, and nine of the 18 scored under 40. Manhattan also holds the highest score in the Index. A plausible reading is that in a dense market a few well-known names absorb the recommendations while the rest are omitted, but this dataset records the pattern without establishing the cause.</p>
</div>
<div class="ta-faq__item">
<h3 class="ta-faq__q">Does this study explain what makes a dispensary visible in ChatGPT?</h3>
<p class="ta-faq__a">No, and that is deliberate. This round recorded what ChatGPT returned. It did not systematically collect the variables that would let anyone test a cause, such as Google Business Profile completeness, review volume and recency, third-party editorial coverage, menu indexability, or name and address consistency across directories. Gathering those against the same 100 businesses is the next piece of work.</p>
