<div class="ta-qa-label">Quick Answer</div>
<p><strong>A website hero video works when it is planned around the page, not added to it afterwards.</strong> Before you commission one, decide what the headline and button will say, how the video should crop on a phone, where the loop will start and end, and how the file will reach the visitor.</p>
<p><strong>What to bring:</strong> the page or a wireframe, your brand references, the platform you use (Framer, Webflow or WordPress) and a target launch date. With these, a production team can plan composition, loop point and delivery files in one pass.</p>

<h2 id="ta-role">What the Hero Video Is For</h2>
<p>The first screen has one job: tell a visitor what this is and what to do next. A video can set the atmosphere and show the product or service in motion, but it should support the headline and the button, not compete with them. If the video needs the viewer's full attention to make sense, it is probably a launch film, not a hero loop. Those are different formats, and we describe both on our <a href="/services/ai-video-production/">AI video production</a> page.</p>
<p>Our own example is the <a href="https://films.tarasovs.me/projects/florist-website-hero-banner">Florist website hero banner</a>, a self-initiated project built to show atmospheric video for the opening screen of a site.</p>
<h2 id="ta-composition">Composition Around the Headline</h2>
<p>Text is read over the video, so the frame needs calm areas. Decide before production where the headline, the sub-text and the button will sit, and keep the busiest movement away from them.</p>
<ul>
<li>Leave a quiet zone for the headline, on the left or in the centre depending on the layout.</li>
<li>Check contrast on the real frames, not just the first one. A dark overlay can help, but it should not flatten the image.</li>
<li>Avoid sudden bright flashes behind white text.</li>
<li>Share the page design or a wireframe at the brief stage, so the composition is built for it.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="ta-mobile">Mobile Crop</h2>
<p>A wide video cut to a tall phone screen loses much of its frame. Plan the crop on day one: which part of the scene must remain visible, and whether the phone version needs its own composition. For some projects a static poster on phones is the better choice, as it keeps the first screen fast and clear.</p>
<ul>
<li>Pick the subject that must stay in view and keep it near the centre.</li>
<li>Ask for a mobile crop or a separate vertical edit if the subject sits at the side.</li>
<li>Always prepare a poster image that looks complete without the video.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="ta-loop">Loop Point and Length</h2>
<p>A background loop should feel continuous. That means the last frame has to lead back into the first without a visible jump, and the movement should be slow enough to repeat many times without tiring the eye.</p>

<h2 id="ta-delivery">Files and Delivery</h2>
<p>Do not publish the production master on your website. A web file is prepared separately, optimised for the page. Ask what you will receive, so the video does not slow the first screen down.</p>
<ul>
<li>A web-optimised video file in a format your platform supports (for example MP4 or WebM).</li>
<li>A poster image shown before the video loads.</li>
<li>A mobile version or crop, if agreed.</li>
<li>Notes on how to place the video on <a href="/services/framer-development/">Framer</a>, Webflow or WordPress, if integration is part of the project.</li>
</ul>
<p>We do not promise a specific PageSpeed score or a conversion lift from adding video. What we can do is keep the files light and test the page before it goes live.</p>
<h2 id="ta-access">Motion and Accessibility</h2>
<ul>
<li>Keep the video muted. Sound should start only when a visitor chooses it.</li>
<li>Respect the browser setting for reduced motion and show the poster instead.</li>
<li>Offer a pause control for any video that plays automatically.</li>
<li>Make sure the text stays readable if the video does not load.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="ta-checklist">Checklist Before You Commission a Hero Video</h2>
<ol>
<li>The headline and button text, or a near-final page design.</li>
<li>The platform: Framer, Webflow, WordPress or another.</li>
<li>Brand references and anything the video must show.</li>
<li>How the video should look on a phone: a crop, a separate edit or a poster.</li>
<li>The loop type and an approximate length.</li>
<li>The files you expect: web video, poster, mobile version.</li>
<li>Who will place the video on the site: you or the production team.</li>
<li>The target launch date.</li>
</ol>
<p>If you are building or redesigning the site at the same time, video can be scoped together with <a href="/services/website-design-services/">website design</a> as one project, so composition, text and page layout are decided together.</p>
