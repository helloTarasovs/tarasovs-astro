<div class="ta-qa-label">Quick Answer</div>
<p><strong>AI video production does not have a standard price, because the cost follows the amount of work, not the minutes of footage.</strong> The main drivers are film length, number of scenes, how exactly a product or character must be reproduced, sound, the number of versions and how many review rounds are agreed.</p>
<p><strong>The practical way to get a reliable quote:</strong> send a short brief with the placement, the target launch date and your references. A good proposal fixes the length, shots, formats, revision stages and delivery files before production begins.</p>

<h2 id="ta-drivers">What Drives the Cost of an AI Video Project</h2>
<p>Many buyers expect AI to make video a fixed, cheap commodity. In practice, the generation of raw material is only one part of the work. Direction, selection, editing, sound and delivery still take time, and the harder the brief, the more material has to be created and discarded before a scene is good enough to use.</p>
<div class="ta-table-wrap">
<table class="ta-table">
<thead>
<tr><th>Factor</th><th>Why it changes the scope</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td class="ta-row-label">Film length and number of scenes</td><td>Every scene needs to be developed, selected and edited. Ten short, simple shots are a different job from ten shots that must match each other.</td></tr>
<tr><td class="ta-row-label">Product or character accuracy</td><td>A generic atmosphere is easy to reproduce. A specific bottle, label, logo or recurring character needs references, checks and sometimes extra finishing.</td></tr>
<tr><td class="ta-row-label">Consistency across scenes</td><td>The same product, person or look must hold from shot to shot. This is where most of the selection and correction time goes.</td></tr>
<tr><td class="ta-row-label">Sound</td><td>Music direction, sound design, a voice-over and the final mix are separate pieces of work and can be included or left out.</td></tr>
<tr><td class="ta-row-label">Formats and versions</td><td>A 16:9 master, a 9:16 cut and a short teaser are different edits. Some placements need a new composition, not a crop.</td></tr>
<tr><td class="ta-row-label">Review rounds</td><td>The number of agreed review stages and how much can change at each one shapes the schedule and the effort.</td></tr>
<tr><td class="ta-row-label">Delivery and web use</td><td>Poster images, web-optimised files, subtitles and integration into a website are scoped separately from the film itself.</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<h2 id="ta-not-cost">What Does Not Change the Price Much</h2>
<p>Some assumptions from traditional production do not carry over directly. Location, crew size, travel and equipment are not the main cost lines in an AI-assisted project. At the same time, "AI" does not mean unlimited iterations for free. A project is priced around a defined result, so a clear scope matters more than a low hourly figure.</p>
<div class="ta-note">We do not publish a price list or typical ranges on this page. Any number given without a brief would be a guess, and a guess is not useful when you are planning a budget.</div>
<h2 id="ta-scope">What a Proposal Should Define Before Production Starts</h2>
<p>Whoever you work with, ask for the following to be written down. This is also how we scope our own <a href="/services/ai-video-production/">AI video production</a> projects.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Film length</strong> and the number of shots.</li>
<li><strong>Formats:</strong> for example 16:9, 9:16, 4:5 or 1:1, and whether each is a separate edit.</li>
<li><strong>Review stages:</strong> what is approved at the concept, the first cut and the final.</li>
<li><strong>Sound:</strong> music, sound design, voice-over and mix, or what is left to you.</li>
<li><strong>Delivery files:</strong> master, versions, posters, subtitles and web files.</li>
<li><strong>Source materials</strong> you provide: product images, packaging artwork, brand guidelines.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="ta-prepare">How to Prepare a Brief That Gets an Accurate Quote</h2>
<p>You do not need a finished concept. A short brief is enough if it answers these questions:</p>
<ol>
<li>What is being promoted: a product, a website, a launch or a brand?</li>
<li>Where will the video be used: a website hero, a product page, social feeds, an advertisement or a presentation?</li>
<li>Who will watch it, and on which device?</li>
<li>When does it need to go live?</li>
<li>Which references do you like, and which brand or product materials can you share?</li>
<li>Which versions do you need: horizontal, vertical, short cut-downs?</li>
</ol>
<p>The placement matters most. A looping background for a website needs a different treatment from a launch film, even at the same length. If the video is for a site, see <a href="/services/ai-video-production/#hero-videos">website hero videos</a> and our article on <a href="/website-hero-video-brief/">what to prepare before you commission one</a>.</p>
<h2 id="ta-compare">How to Compare Quotes Fairly</h2>
<p>Two quotes for "a 30-second video" can describe very different work. Compare them on content, not on the headline number.</p>
<ul>
<li>Do both include the same number of formats and versions?</li>
<li>Is sound included, and at what level?</li>
<li>How many review rounds are agreed, and what counts as a change versus a new scene?</li>
<li>Does the quote say how a specific product or logo will be handled?</li>
<li>Are web files, posters and subtitles part of delivery?</li>
</ul>
<p>Look at relevant work, too. Our <a href="https://films.tarasovs.me/">film portfolio</a> shows commissioned and self-initiated projects with their status stated, so you can judge the approach on real examples such as <a href="https://films.tarasovs.me/projects/meridian">Meridian</a> and <a href="https://films.tarasovs.me/projects/centra-datacore">Centra DataCore</a>.</p>
