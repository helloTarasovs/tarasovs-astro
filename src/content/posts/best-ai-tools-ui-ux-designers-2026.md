<p><strong>The best AI tools for UI/UX designers in August 2026 are Figma (AI now bundled into paid plans), Framer (design-to-live-site), Google Stitch (free text-to-UI, successor to the now-dead Galileo AI), UX Pilot, Uizard, Visily, v0 by Vercel, and Relume.</strong> Prices range from free to $100/month depending on how much AI generation you actually use.</p>
<p>The part most "best AI tools" lists skip: this market turns over fast, and lists go stale quickly. Our own <a href="/10-best-ai-tools-for-ui-ux-designers-in-2024/">2024 list</a> is a case in point, and we'll show you exactly how below. Treat any list like this, including this one, as a snapshot, not a permanent recommendation.</p>

<h2 id="tools">The tools worth using right now</h2>
<p>This isn't a ranked top 10. It's the shortlist of tools that are actively maintained, priced transparently, and solve a distinct enough problem that they're not redundant with each other. Screenshots and generic feature lists are easy to find; what's harder to find is what each tool actually costs once you're past the free tier, and where it breaks.</p>
<div class="ta-img">
<img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/ai-design-tools-workflow.webp" alt="Where each AI design tool fits in a typical UI/UX workflow, from wireframing to a live site" width="1200" height="675">
</div>
<div class="ta-tool">
<div class="ta-tool__head">
<h3 class="ta-tool__name">Figma (with Figma Make and Weave)</h3>
<span class="ta-tool__price">$0–$90/mo</span>
</div>
<p>Still the default place most design work happens. AI features (Make for prompt-to-prototype, Weave for the newer generative tooling announced at Config 2026) are now bundled into paid plans rather than sold separately, so if your team already pays for Figma Professional, you already have this.</p>
<p class="ta-tool__take">The catch is credits, not price. Professional includes 3,000 AI credits a month; heavy generation use drains that fast, and extra credits are a paid add-on on every tier.</p>
</div>
<div class="ta-tool">
<div class="ta-tool__head">
<h3 class="ta-tool__name">Framer</h3>
<span class="ta-tool__price">$0–$30/mo</span>
</div>
<p>The one tool on this list that takes you from AI-assisted design straight to a live, hosted site, no handoff to a developer required. We've used it directly for client work; see our <a href="/connect-claude-to-framer/">Connect Claude to Framer</a> and <a href="/higgsfield-claude-framer-ai-video/">Higgsfield AI video in Framer</a> pieces for what that actually looks like in practice, including what breaks.</p>
<p class="ta-tool__take">Billing is per site, so five client sites means five separate subscriptions. Extra editors are $20 each on top. Framer's Pro Expert programme does offer discounts, including free editor access on client projects, if you qualify.</p>
</div>
<div class="ta-tool">
<div class="ta-tool__head">
<h3 class="ta-tool__name">Google Stitch</h3>
<span class="ta-tool__price">Free (for now)</span>
</div>
<p>Type a description, get a full UI screen with realistic content and usable components. This is the direct successor to Galileo AI, which Google acquired and folded into Stitch in May 2025. It's currently free while it lives inside Google Labs (350 standard generations a month plus 200 experimental), with paid plans expected once it graduates out of the Labs stage.</p>
<p class="ta-tool__take">Free and good is a great combination until the day it isn't. Google Labs products get killed or repriced without much warning; this is worth remembering given what happened to the tool it replaced.</p>
</div>
<div class="ta-tool">
<div class="ta-tool__head">
<h3 class="ta-tool__name">UX Pilot</h3>
<span class="ta-tool__price">$0–$22/mo</span>
</div>
<p>Leans into UX structure rather than visual polish: wireframes, multi-screen flows, and usability reasoning from a prompt, with export to Figma. Useful earlier in a project than most of the tools here, when you're still figuring out the flow rather than the look.</p>
<p class="ta-tool__take">Pricing is credit-metered. The $12/month plan gets you roughly 70 screens a month; go over and you're buying more credits or waiting for the reset.</p>
</div>
<div class="ta-tool">
<div class="ta-tool__head">
<h3 class="ta-tool__name">Uizard</h3>
<span class="ta-tool__price">$0–$39/mo</span>
</div>
<p>One of the few tools that made our 2024 list and is still recognizably the same product two years later: prompt-to-UI, screenshot-to-editable-mockup, drag-and-drop editing on top. That kind of stability is rare enough in this market to count as a point in its favor.</p>
<p class="ta-tool__take">The free plan is thin: three AI generations a month on the older Autodesigner 1.5 engine, so you'll hit the paywall almost immediately if you're evaluating it seriously. Truly unlimited generation is Enterprise-only; the $39 Business tier caps at 5,000 a month.</p>
</div>
<div class="ta-tool">
<div class="ta-tool__head">
<h3 class="ta-tool__name">Visily</h3>
<span class="ta-tool__price">$0–$29/mo</span>
</div>
<p>Built for teams where not everyone is a designer: no learning curve, prebuilt templates, and AI that turns a text prompt, screenshot, or sketch into an editable wireframe. Viewer seats are free, only editors count toward your bill, which matters if you have a lot of stakeholders who just need to comment.</p>
<p class="ta-tool__take">The free tier watermarks every export, so it's a trial, not a real free plan for shipping anything.</p>
</div>
<div class="ta-tool">
<div class="ta-tool__head">
<h3 class="ta-tool__name">v0 by Vercel</h3>
<span class="ta-tool__price">$0–$100/mo</span>
</div>
<p>Sits on the design/dev border more than any other tool here: prompt in, working React and Tailwind components out, deployable straight to Vercel. Billing is token-based rather than a flat per-generation credit, so cost depends on prompt complexity and which model you use.</p>
<p class="ta-tool__take">This is a tool for teams that want to skip the design file entirely and go straight to code. If your workflow still needs a Figma handoff step, it's the wrong tool.</p>
</div>
<div class="ta-tool">
<div class="ta-tool__head">
<h3 class="ta-tool__name">Relume</h3>
<span class="ta-tool__price">Free, paid tiers vary</span>
</div>
<p>AI sitemap and wireframe generation paired with a large Webflow, Figma, and React component library, over 1,000 components on paid plans. Strongest fit for agencies already building in Webflow.</p>
<p class="ta-tool__take">We couldn't get a single consistent number for the entry paid tier across sources at the time of writing; Relume's own pricing page doesn't display a plain dollar figure without signing in. Confirm current pricing directly before quoting a client.</p>
</div>

<h3>Our own 2024 list is the clearest example</h3>
<p>We published a <a href="/10-best-ai-tools-for-ui-ux-designers-in-2024/">list of ten AI tools for UI/UX designers</a> in April 2024. One of them, VisualEyes, was already gone when we published it. Neurons had acquired VisualEyes and Loceye in June 2022, and the standalone VisualEyes product ceased operating in January 2023, more than a year before our article went live. <p>We're leaving that in rather than quietly deleting it, because it makes the point better than any statistic could: this research goes stale faster than most people writing it realise, and a plausible-sounding tool recommendation can be out of date before it's even published. That includes recommendations in this article.</p>
<p>None of this means the tools listed above are about to disappear. It means don't build a workflow, or a client deliverable process, that assumes any single AI design tool will still exist in its current form two years from now.</p>
<h2 id="pick">How to pick a tool that survives past next year</h2>
<p><strong>Prefer tools bundled into a platform you already pay for.</strong> Figma's AI features and Framer's AI features are less likely to vanish outright than a standalone startup's, because killing them means killing revenue the parent company already has.</p>
<p><strong>Check whether the company has a real business model, not just funding.</strong> CodeParrot had YC backing and real usage and still shut down because it couldn't raise a follow-on round. A tool selling subscriptions to paying users is on steadier ground than one burning venture funding while offering a generous free tier.</p>
<p><strong>Be skeptical of anything still labeled "Labs" or "experimental," even from Google or OpenAI.</strong> Google Stitch is free and genuinely useful right now, and it is also explicitly a Google Labs product, the same category Google has quietly discontinued things from before.</p>
<p><strong>Make sure you can export.</strong> Tools that let you export to Figma, code, or standard formats leave you with something even if the tool itself disappears tomorrow. Tools that lock your work inside a proprietary editor don't.</p>
<p><strong>Don't build your entire process around one tool's specific prompt syntax or workflow.</strong> If switching tools means retraining your whole team from scratch, you've taken on more risk than the subscription price suggests.</p>
<h2 id="when">What we'd actually use, by situation</h2>
<p><strong>Early-stage exploration, no budget yet:</strong> Google Stitch or Uizard's free tier. Fast enough to test an idea, cheap enough to throw away.</p>
<p><strong>Structuring a flow before you touch visuals:</strong> UX Pilot. It's built around UX logic first, which most of the prompt-to-screen tools skip.</p>
<p><strong>Non-designers on the team need to contribute wireframes:</strong> Visily. The no-learning-curve pitch is legitimate for stakeholders who aren't going to learn Figma.</p>
<p><strong>Taking a design straight to a live, hosted site:</strong> Framer. It's the only tool on this list where "finished" means published, not handed off.</p>
<p><strong>Handing off straight to React code instead of a design file:</strong> v0 by Vercel, if your team is comfortable skipping the traditional design handoff entirely.</p>
<p><strong>You already pay for Figma:</strong> use what's already bundled in before adding another subscription. It's the least likely tool on this list to disappear.</p>
